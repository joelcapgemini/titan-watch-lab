# Interview: WSDOT traffic management centre operator

Label: role-play, not evidence. Pre-seeded by the facilitator. Method 1b.

## Who

Operator on shift at the WSDOT traffic management centre. Physically closes the span: variable message signs, lane signals, the entry gates at both ends. Sits in a room in Shoreline with a wall of cameras. Has closed the span for wind, never for anything that moves.

## Can see

Cameras at both ends and mid-span, with gaps between them. Loop detectors giving flow per lane. Sign and signal state. Gate state once a person on the ground confirms it. Phone to State Patrol, rail control and the regional duty officer.

## Cannot see

A count of people. Which vehicles are stuck. Terrakon. Anchor strain; that is the engineers' screen, in a different building. The Titan Watch threat board.

## Questions and answers

**When a closure order arrives, what happens in the first five minutes?**
Signs to closed, two minutes. Lane signals red, one minute, in parallel. Gates at both ends down, three minutes, and someone has to drive to each gate and confirm it, so call it four before I trust them. One call to rail control, one to State Patrol for the ramps. About four minutes before anything on the road has changed. Assume and label: four.

**How long to clear the span, first action to confirmed empty?**
Fifteen to twenty minutes at commute load. Eight to ten when it is light. The drain is the slow part, not my buttons. Then a camera sweep to confirm empty, five to ten minutes on its own, because the span is long and the cameras have gaps. We have never done it against a clock. Assume and label: eighteen, plus the sweep.

**How many people are on the span right now?**
I do not know. The loops give me flow, not count. I can tell you about 4,000 vehicles by multiplying lanes and length and guessing the gap. I cannot tell you how many are in them, and I cannot tell you about the trains at all; rail control knows that.

**What do you need before you push the first button?**
A deadline I believe, from someone I recognise, as a phone call with a time in it. Not a threat level. Not a news report. If the time changes I need to be told it changed, not find out.

**What would you want from a tool?**
One countdown, on the wall I already look at, that says how old it is. And one list of what is still on the span, in order from the west end, with the stuck ones flagged. Not a map. I have forty cameras; I do not need another picture.

## Coach observations

- Four minutes before anything on the road changes, then 15 to 20 to drain, then 5 to 10 to confirm. The chain is close to 30 minutes against a 26-minute clock that may be shorter. Q1 is a margin problem before it is anything else.
- "Flow, not count" is the span contents gap (Q5) from the side that owns the cameras. The TMC does not know what it is clearing.
- "Told it changed, not find out" is the first ask for a countdown that announces its own moves. It will recur.
