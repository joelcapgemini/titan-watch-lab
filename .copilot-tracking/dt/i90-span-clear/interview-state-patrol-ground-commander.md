# Interview: State Patrol ground commander, I-90 ramps

Label: role-play, not evidence. Pre-seeded by the facilitator. Method 1b.

## Who

Sergeant running the State Patrol units nearest the span. Blocks the ramps on the ground when the TMC closes the road. Reports to the incident commander. Has closed I-90 for crashes and snow, never against a clock.

## Can see

Four units and their positions. The ramps once a unit is on them. Traffic from the shoulder. The radio with dispatch and the TMC.

## Cannot see

Anything on the span itself beyond the first few hundred metres. Terrakon. The countdown. Which vehicles are stuck. Whether the gates are down until the TMC says so.

## Questions and answers

**When the order comes, how fast are the ramps blocked?**
Three to five minutes with four units, if they are nearby. On a commute they are nearby because they are already working crashes. One unit per ramp, Seattle side first because that is where the inflow is. Assume and label: five.

**What about the people already on the span?**
Nothing. I cannot chase a driver onto a closing bridge, and I cannot walk two kilometres of stopped traffic telling people to leave their cars. Once the ramps are shut, everyone already past them is on their own until they reach the far end.

**What do you need before you act?**
A time from a voice I recognise. The commander, the TMC duty officer, dispatch. If Titan Watch called me I would ask who they were. I have never heard of a Titan Watch order and I would not know how to verify one.

**What happens if the closure is wrong?**
We reversed one once, for weather. A day of complaints and a story in the paper. Against a kaiju a false alarm costs the same day. A late closure costs the bridge and everyone on it. Those are not the same size and the design should know it.

**What would you want?**
To hear the time change the moment it changes, on the radio, not after. And to know when the span is confirmed empty so I can let an ambulance through if one comes. Today I would find out both by asking.

## Coach observations

- Five minutes to block the ramps in role-play against the TMC's four. Conflict logged, not resolved. Either way inflow continues for most of five minutes after the order.
- State Patrol cannot reach anyone already on the span. The people most at risk are outside every control in the chain once the gates drop.
- The asymmetry, false alarm costs a day and late closure costs the bridge, is a design principle offered by a specialist. Carry it to the commander interview.
