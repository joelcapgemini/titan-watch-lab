# Scope boundaries: i90-span-clear

Pre-seeded by the facilitator. Method 1a complete. Participants do not redo this; they add interviews under their own slug and the lead synthesises.

## Problem statement, as currently understood

Terrakon will reach the I-90 floating span in less than 26 minutes. About 4,000 vehicles and possibly two light-rail trains are on it. Clearing it takes 15 to 20 minutes at commute load and the order takes 4 to 5 minutes to reach the road. Nobody today has, in one place, when the span becomes unsafe, what is on it, and what Mercer Island has been told.

## Outcome

Starting outcome, fixed for every scenario: Save as much human life as possible, then prevent as much public property damage as possible, in that order. A software tool is expected to come out of this.

Restated outcome, after synthesis: _(coach writes it here; the line above stays)_

## In scope

- The 26 minutes from the Titan Watch alert to Terrakon reaching the anchor line, whatever that turns out to mean.
- The decision chain from alert to gates down, trains held, ramps blocked and the span confirmed empty.
- Telling Mercer Island what is happening and why, before it is cut off.
- The information each decider needs and does not have.
- A software tool is expected to come out of this. Which part of the chain it serves is open.

## Out of scope

- Engaging or deflecting Terrakon.
- Island shelter and evacuation operations after the island has been told.
- Rebuilding or repairing the span.
- Anything that assumes Terrakon changes course.

## Boundary questions to revisit

- Q1: How long does an order take to reach the people who act, and a report to reach the commander? The commander has full authority; the gap is lag and information.
- Q2: Does "anchor line" mean the span is unsafe (T1), the span fails (T2), or Terrakon reaches the island shoreline?
- Q3: Where does the tool stop caring: when the span is confirmed empty, or when Mercer Island has been told what and why?
