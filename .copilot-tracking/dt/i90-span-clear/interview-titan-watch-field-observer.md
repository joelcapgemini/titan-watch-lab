# Interview: Titan Watch field observer, west shore

Label: role-play, not evidence. Pre-seeded by the facilitator. Method 1b.

## Who

Field observer posted on high ground on the Seattle side of the lake with optics and a radio. Reports what Terrakon is doing to the duty analyst. Has watched three transits. Has never been asked about a bridge.

## Can see

Terrakon itself: posture, pace, the way it moves its head before it turns, the spray when a footfall lands in deeper water. The span, as a line across the lake.

## Cannot see

Position better than about a kilometre; there is nothing to range against on open water. The satellite track. The countdown. Anything on the span.

## Questions and answers

**What do you see that the satellite does not?**
Behaviour. The satellite gives a dot every 30 seconds. I can tell you it is about to change pace about a minute before the dots show it, because it drops its shoulders. I can tell you it is slowing to turn, which it does not do on a transit, so if it does, something is wrong. Assume and label: one minute of warning on a pace change.

**Can you tell me when it reaches the bridge?**
No. I can tell you when it is close, as a guess, and I can tell you when it touches, because I will see the span move. By then it is too late to be useful.

**Who do you report to?**
The duty analyst, by radio. Nobody else. If the analyst is on another call, my report waits. I have no number for the bridge and I would not know who to ask for.

**Has the estimate been wrong in your experience?**
Early, every time. It speeds up in the last kilometre before shore, and the straight-line figure does not know that. The analyst knows it; the board does not show it.

**What would you want?**
A way to put "pace change, now" into whatever the bridge people are looking at, with my name on it, without going through the analyst's queue. One button. I do not need the picture. I am looking at the picture.

## Coach observations

- The observer sees behaviour a minute ahead of the satellite but has no path to the people who would act on it. Q1 has a lag at the sensing end as well as the ordering end.
- Satellite sees position; the observer sees behaviour; the engineer sees strain. Three sources, three organisations, no common view. H1 narrows toward a join, not a new sensor.
- "With my name on it" is a request for attribution on every input to the countdown. It matches the grading rule about recorded decisions.
