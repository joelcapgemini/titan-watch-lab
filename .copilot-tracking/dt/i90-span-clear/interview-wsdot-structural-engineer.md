# Interview: WSDOT structural engineer, floating bridges

Label: role-play, not evidence. Pre-seeded by the facilitator. Method 1b.

## Who

Duty structural engineer for the state's floating bridges. Owns the anchor cable ratings and the pontoon tolerances. Is the person who says the span is unsafe. Sits at a screen of strain and tilt readings in a different building from the TMC.

## Can see

Strain on each anchor cable, pontoon tilt, wind and wave readings, refreshed every ten seconds. Assume and label: ten. The rating sheet for each cable. Phone to the TMC and the regional duty officer.

## Cannot see

Terrakon. Where it is, how fast, or that it exists. Traffic on the span except as load. The Titan Watch threat board; nobody in this building has a login.

## Questions and answers

**When you say the span is lost, what actually happens?**
Two thresholds, not one event. T1, unsafe for traffic: pontoon movement or anchor strain past tolerance, and I close it. T2, structural failure: cables part, pontoons drift, the span breaks up. The gap between them is minutes to tens of minutes, and it depends on load. A full span fails sooner than an empty one. Assume and label: ten minutes at commute load, thirty empty.

**When does T1 arrive, with Terrakon walking the lake bed?**
Before it touches us. Every footfall puts a pressure wave through the water and the pontoons ride it. The cables see that as cyclic strain. I would expect T1 somewhere ahead of contact; I cannot say how far ahead because I do not know where it is or how fast it is coming. Assume and label: five minutes before contact.

**Who tells you where it is?**
Nobody. I would hear it on the radio like everyone else. There is no channel between this room and Titan Watch, and I would not know who to call there. I know load, not cause.

**Could you compute T1 if you had the track?**
Roughly, yes, with the rating sheet and the current traffic load, in a few minutes with a spreadsheet. Nobody has asked, and nobody has built it. Today I call T1 when I see it on the gauges, which is too late to clear anything.

**What would you want from a tool?**
Their track on my screen, or my strain on theirs. One of us has to see both. Then a T1 estimate that moves as the load changes, and never moves later without saying why.

## Coach observations

- T1 is the real deadline and nobody computes it. Titan Watch has the track, this room has the strain data, and there is no channel. Q4 is the central gap and it is a joining problem, not a sensing one.
- A loaded span fails sooner. The vehicles the commander is trying to save are shortening the time available to save them.
- "Never moves later without saying why" is the second interview to ask for an honest countdown. It is now a requirement candidate.
