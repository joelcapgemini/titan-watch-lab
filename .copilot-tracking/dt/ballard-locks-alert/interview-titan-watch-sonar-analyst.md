# Interview: Titan Watch sonar analyst

Label: role-play, not evidence. Pre-seeded by the facilitator. Method 1b.

## Who

Sonar analyst at the Project Titan Watch command centre. Processes the submerged returns in the sensor grid and writes them to the log. Has no authority to raise anything. Has never had a submerged kaiju before.

## Can see

Raw returns: position, depth, rough size. The sensor-grid log as it fills. The spacing between returns.

## Cannot see

What the return is. Where it goes between returns. The lock gates. Whether anyone downstream is reading the log.

## Questions and answers

**What does the log actually show?**
A string of returns in Puget Sound with negative elevation, so below the surface, moving roughly east. Then gaps. The last clean one was off Shilshole. Since then two returns I would not put my name to and one I would. Assume and label: three good returns in the last hour, four doubtful.

**Could it be something else?**
Yes. Grey whales come into the Sound. Submarines leave Bangor and do not tell us. Something 88 m long should be obvious and it is not, because a serpent lying still on the bottom looks like the bottom. I cannot tell a whale from a kaiju from one return.

**How confident are you, right now?**
I do not have a scale. We report a return or we do not. If you asked me to put a number on it I would say sixty percent it is Skarnyx, and I would want that written down as my guess, not Titan Watch's finding. Assume and label: sixty.

**When is the next return?**
When the grid gets one. Minutes, or not at all. There is no refresh rate on a thing that chooses when to move.

**What would you want to give the commander?**
A return with a confidence beside it and the reason for the confidence: size matches, depth matches, nothing else known to be there. And a record of what else was there, so the whale can be ruled out by someone who knows whales. I do not.

## Coach observations

- Titan Watch has no confidence scale. "A return or no return" is the all-or-nothing problem one step before the alert.
- Sixty percent is a role-play guess the analyst does not want attributed to the organisation. Confidence has an ownership problem as well as a scale problem.
- "Someone who knows whales" points at the fisheries biologist. The evidence to rule out a false alarm exists and it is not in the sonar room.
