# Interview: Kirkland emergency manager

Label: role-play, not evidence. Pre-seeded by the facilitator. Method 1b.

## Who

Emergency manager for the City of Kirkland, on the eastern shore of Lake Washington. Small team, one duty phone. Shares the lake with Seattle and Bellevue and hears from Seattle when Seattle remembers. Speaks for Bellevue here; the facilitator assumes the same picture from the south shore.

## Can see

The Titan Watch board, same as everyone. The Kirkland waterfront, in person from the office window. The county alert request form.

## Cannot see

Sonar. The locks, which are on the far side of the lake and a different county agency. Whether Seattle has asked for an alert yet.

## Questions and answers

**What have you been told?**
Seattle called and said there is a sonar return in Puget Sound and they are not sure. That is all. I looked at the board and it says submerged. There is a lake between me and the locks and nobody has told me how long that lake takes to cross or whether the thing even gets through.

**What does the alert look like from your end?**
The county sends one message to one polygon. If Seattle asks for it, it probably covers us whether I agree or not. If I ask for it alone, the county will ask why Seattle has not. There is no version where I warn my shoreline quietly.

**What would you want to be able to do that is less than an alert?**
Close the parks and the marina. Pull the swim beaches. Tell the harbour patrol to move people off the docks. None of that needs a wireless alert. All of it needs me to know something I do not know. Assume and label: an hour to clear the marina with the staff I have.

**What does a false alarm cost Kirkland?**
A summer evening on the waterfront, and the next one. We did a tsunami test once and the complaints lasted a week. Nobody has costed it. I would not know where to write it down.

**What do you need?**
To see the same evidence Seattle sees, at the same time, not after Seattle has decided. And a way to act small before anyone acts big.

## Coach observations

- Kirkland wants graded action: close the marina, pull the beaches, before an alert. The all-or-nothing problem is on the action side, not only the message side.
- The lake crossing time is unknown to the person on the far shore. There is no clock in the scenario and the one person who would benefit from a partial one does not have it.
- "Same evidence, same time" repeats the Seattle duty officer's ask from the receiving end.
