# Interview: Seattle fire battalion chief, stadium detail

Label: role-play, not evidence. Pre-seeded by the facilitator. Method 1b.

## Who

Battalion chief for the event. Positions engines and medic units for whatever goes wrong at a gate, on a stair or in the bowl. Staged on the east side. Reports through fire dispatch; works alongside the police lead.

## Can see

Own units and crews. The fire dispatch channel. The east gate line from the staging area. The stadium's fire alarm panel, if someone in the operations centre reads it out.

## Cannot see

The bowl. The west and north gates. Gorathos. The clock, except through dispatch, who hear it from the stadium.

## Questions and answers

**What changes for you when 68,000 people move at once instead of over an hour?**
Everything. I stage for one incident at a time, a fall on a stair or a heart on the concourse. A held gate with a crowd pushing from behind is a crush, and a crush at two gates at once is more than I have. I have four medic units. I can cover two gate lines properly. Assume and label: two.

**What does a crush look like from where you stand?**
It starts when a gate holds and the people at the back do not know why. Two minutes of pushing is enough to put people on the ground. I have seen it on a normal night. The cure is the gate ahead opening or the crowd behind stopping, and neither is mine to do.

**What happens if Gorathos reaches the stadium while you have crews at a gate?**
I pull them. I do not know how much warning I would get. I hear the clock from dispatch, who hear it from the stadium, who hear it from the liaison. Four people. By the time it reaches me I would not trust the number.

**Has anything like this been drilled?**
Full evacuation, once, with a few hundred volunteers and no clock. It took an afternoon and we still had a medic unit on the wrong side of the building. Shelter in place for lightning, which is the opposite drill.

**What would you want from a tool?**
A countdown I can trust and the gate list with anything held flagged, because a held gate is where I will be needed. Not a map of the monster. A list of gates.

## Coach observations

- The clock reaches fire and rescue through four people. The chain has a known lag at the end as well as the start, and nobody along it carries the age of the number.
- A held gate is a crush waiting to happen; a released gate facing the track is worse. The per-gate decision has a casualty cost in both directions, which is the hard choice at gate scale.
- "Not a map, a list of gates" matches the operations director and the gate supervisor. Three interviews want the same artefact for three reasons.
