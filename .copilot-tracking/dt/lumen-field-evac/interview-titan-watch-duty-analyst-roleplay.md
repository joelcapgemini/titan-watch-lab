# Interview: Titan Watch duty analyst

Label: role-play, not evidence. Pre-seeded by the facilitator. Method 1b.

## Who

Duty analyst at the Project Titan Watch command centre. Produces the track estimate and the 11-minute figure. Has no stadium authority and has never been inside Lumen Field on an event night.

## Can see

The sensor grid: position, heading, speed for Gorathos every 30 seconds. The threat board. The sensor log at `data/sensor-grid.txt`. The roster entry at `data/monsters/gorathos.json`.

## Cannot see

The stadium. Gates. Streets. Whether anyone at the stadium is reading the threat board.

## Questions and answers

**Where does 11 minutes come from?**
Distance from the last ping to the point where Gorathos's projected track meets the seawall at Alaskan Way, divided by 34.5 km/h from the roster. Straight line. We call that point the seawall because it is where the water stops. Whether the stadium is at risk at that moment or two minutes later, I cannot say. That is a stadium question.

**How much does it move?**
Not much on speed; it does not sprint. It moves on heading. The world rules say it is drawn to dense noise and light. We have seen the track bend in earlier pings toward the brightest thing on the shore. Tonight the brightest thing on the shore is the stadium. Assume and label: the seawall time is firm within a minute; where it goes after the seawall is not.

**What do you tell, and to whom?**
We raise the threat condition on the board and send a citywide alert when it crosses a threshold. The stadium has a liaison who watches the board. Whether the liaison can reach the gates in time, I do not know. We have no line to the stadium, the police or the trains.

**Has the estimate been wrong?**
On time, usually. On heading, yes. It arrived where the light was, not where the straight line said. Assume and label: one landfall in three bends toward light in the last kilometre.

**What would you want to give the stadium?**
Not a number. The time to the seawall and the time to the stadium as two figures, the heading with how much it has bent in the last three pings, and a flag when a ping is missed so nobody trusts a stale one.

## Coach observations

- "Seawall" is a geographer's phrase. Q2 is a definition problem: the analyst can say when it reaches the water, not when the stadium is at risk.
- The clock is firm and the heading is not. Any plan that marks gates as facing or not facing the kaiju rests on a heading that bends toward the stadium's own noise.
- Titan Watch has no line to the stadium, police or trains. Same organisational gap as every other interview, from the sensor side.
- Two figures, a bend indicator and a staleness flag. That is a requirement, if it survives the other interviews.
