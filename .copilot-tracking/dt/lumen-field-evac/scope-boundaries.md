# Scope boundaries: lumen-field-evac

Pre-seeded by the facilitator. Method 1a complete. Participants do not redo this; they add interviews under their own slug and the lead synthesises.

## Problem statement, as currently understood

Gorathos will reach the seawall in 11 minutes. Lumen Field is sold out, 68,000 people inside, and a full clear takes longer than that on a normal night with every gate open. Some gates open toward the kaiju and some away from it. Nobody today has, in one place, which gates are clear, which face Gorathos, who has been told to hold or release each one, and how old the clock is.

## Outcome

Starting outcome, fixed for every scenario: Save as much human life as possible, then prevent as much public property damage as possible, in that order. A software tool is expected to come out of this.

Restated outcome, after synthesis: _(coach writes it here; the line above stays)_

## In scope

- The 11 minutes from the Titan Watch alert to Gorathos reaching the seawall, and the two minutes after that to the stadium.
- The decision chain from alert to every gate and every section having an instruction.
- The information each decider needs and does not have.
- A software tool is expected to come out of this. Which part of the chain it serves is open.

## Out of scope

- Engaging or deflecting Gorathos.
- Evacuating downtown, the waterfront or the rest of SoDo.
- Recovery after Gorathos has passed.
- Anything that assumes Gorathos stops.

## Boundary questions to revisit

- Q1: How long does an order take to reach the people who act, and a report to reach the commander? The commander has full authority; the gap is lag and information.
- Q2: Does "seawall" mean the stadium is at risk at 11 minutes, at 13, or only if the track holds?
- Q3: What does the tool stop caring about once a person is "outside the corridor"?
