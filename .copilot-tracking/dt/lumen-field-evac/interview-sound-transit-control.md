# Interview: Sound Transit link control, Stadium station

Label: role-play, not evidence. Pre-seeded by the facilitator. Method 1b.

## Who

Controller at the light rail operations centre. Runs the trains through Stadium station on the east side of the stadium. Has moved post-game crowds many times. Has never been told to run trains against a clock.

## Can see

Every train's position. Platform cameras at Stadium and the next stations. Platform crowding, roughly, from the cameras. The citywide public alert.

## Cannot see

The stadium gates. Which gate feeds the platform. Gorathos. Whether the stadium is sending people to the platform or away from it.

## Questions and answers

**How many people can you move from Stadium station in the next fifteen minutes?**
A train every six minutes each direction on event night. Eight hundred a train if they pack. Southbound only matters tonight; northbound runs into downtown under the track. Three trains south in fifteen minutes, call it two and a half thousand. Assume and label: two thousand five hundred, southbound only.

**How much warning do you need?**
Ten minutes to stage extra trains south of the station and turn the northbound ones. Less than that and the first crowd arrives at a normal platform with a normal train. Nobody has called us.

**Who tells you the stadium is emptying?**
The platform cameras, usually. The crowd arrives and we know. The stadium has our number; they have never used it on a normal night and I do not know if they have it tonight. We find out person by person.

**What happens if the stadium sends twenty thousand people to a platform that holds two thousand?**
The platform fills, the stairs fill, the crowd backs up into the street, and the gate above it is now a held gate whether the stadium meant it or not. That is the failure mode, and it happens on the east side, which is the side everyone has been told is safe.

**What would help?**
One message from the stadium, early: how many are coming to the platform, from which gate, starting when. Even an estimate. We plan against an estimate; we cannot plan against silence. And a way to tell the stadium "platform full, hold that gate" that reaches the gate, not a desk.

## Coach observations

- Transit capacity is finite and the stadium has never counted it. The safe side has a bottleneck that turns a released gate into a held one without anyone deciding.
- Stadium station hears of a crowd when it arrives. Same pattern as Portland at Sea-Tac: open is not the same as ready.
- "Platform full, hold that gate" is a third input to the gate board, from below the street, alongside the police officer at the corner.
- "One message, early, even an estimate" matches the fire chief and the police lead. Same artefact, fourth place.
