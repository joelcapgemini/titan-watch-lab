# Interview: Seattle police incident lead, stadium district

Label: role-play, not evidence. Pre-seeded by the facilitator. Method 1b.

## Who

Lieutenant running the event detail. Owns the streets around the stadium: closures, intersections, officers on foot. Sits in a command vehicle on the south side of the stadium. Has handled a crowd of 68,000 many times, never under a clock.

## Can see

Street cameras at the main intersections. Officers' radio reports. The crowd on the ground once it is outside. The Titan Watch public alert, same as everyone.

## Cannot see

Inside the stadium. Which gates are open. Gorathos, except from an officer on the waterfront with a phone. How old the clock is.

## Questions and answers

**Which streets can take the crowd?**
South and east, toward the ballpark and the busway. Not north, that is downtown and the track. Not west, that is the water. I can close First Avenue and Alaskan Way in six minutes if my people are already at the corners. They are not; they are where the parking is, which is north and west. Assume and label: six minutes to turn the streets, ten to reposition.

**What happens when a crowd comes out of a gate with no direction?**
They walk toward their cars. Tonight that is north and west. They will walk into the thing. Not because they are foolish; because that is where they parked and nobody told them otherwise. A crowd with no instruction follows habit.

**How do you hear the clock?**
The stadium calls me. The stadium hears it from the liaison. I do not know who the liaison hears it from. By the time a number reaches my radio it has been through three people and I do not know how old it is.

**Can you tell the stadium which gates to open?**
I can tell them which streets are clear. They decide gates. Those are two different lists and nobody is holding them against each other. If they open East 4 and I have not cleared Occidental, East 4 empties into a traffic jam.

**What would help?**
The gate list and the street list on one page, with the track drawn on it, updated when either changes. And a way for my officer at the corner to say "this street is blocked" so the gate above it holds without me making a phone call.

## Coach observations

- Streets and gates are two lists held by two organisations and reconciled by phone. The seam is the same shape as fuel and position at Sea-Tac.
- Repositioning officers takes ten minutes of an eleven-minute clock. The window to shape the crowd is shorter than the clock; it is where the officers already stand.
- "A crowd with no instruction follows habit" is silence as an instruction, on foot. The direction of habit is toward the track.
- The officer-at-the-corner report is a second input to the gate board, from outside the building.
