# Interview: Titan Watch liaison at Sea-Tac

Label: role-play, not evidence. Pre-seeded by the facilitator. Method 1b.

## Can see

The Titan Watch threat board, refreshed every 30 seconds. Public weather. The duty manager across the room.

## Cannot see

Aircraft. Whether anyone in the tower has heard. Fuel, runways, authority.

## Questions and answers

**What do you do when the board says 19 minutes?**
I walk to the duty manager and say it. Then I go back to the board. If it changes, I walk again. There is no other channel. Assume and label: thirty seconds per walk, and I miss a refresh each time.

**Do you trust 19?**
No. It is straight-line and there is a front south-west. I would tell anyone who asked to plan for 15. Nobody has asked.

**What does "outer marker" mean?**
I do not know. It is on the board because someone used the phrase. I asked the command centre once and they said it was the extended centreline crossing. The tower may mean something else. That is not my call.

**Can you tell the tower directly?**
I have no line to them and no standing to give them anything. If I called, they would ask who I was.

**What would you want?**
To put the board's number, with its range and its age, on a screen the tower and approach already look at. Not a new screen. Theirs.

## Coach observations

- The clock reaches the airport by a person walking across a room. That is the first link in the chain and it is the slowest relative to its importance.
- The liaison would say 15, not 19, if asked. Nobody asks. The early-edge requirement from the analyst interview is confirmed from the receiving side.
- "Not a new screen, theirs" is the third interview to reject a new display. Integration into existing displays is a constraint, not a preference.
