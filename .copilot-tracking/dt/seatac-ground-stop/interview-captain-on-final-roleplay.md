# Interview: airline captain, number six on final

Label: role-play, not evidence. Pre-seeded by the facilitator. Method 1b.

## Who

Captain of a narrow-body with 162 on board, six aircraft back on final for 16L. Forty minutes of fuel. Has heard a ground stop is coming and nothing else.

## Can see

Own instruments, own fuel, traffic ahead on the display. The approach frequency. Weather out of the window.

## Cannot see

Why the stop. Vespyra. What the aircraft ahead have been told. Whether Portland will take them.

## Questions and answers

**What do you know right now?**
Approach said expect a ground stop. That is all. I have 40 minutes of fuel, which is Portland with a thin margin, or Boeing Field with a fat one. I have not been asked which.

**What would you do if told to divert now?**
Ask where. If the answer takes more than thirty seconds I pick Boeing Field myself and tell them. I will not fly a hold while someone decides.

**What would you do if told nothing?**
Land. I am cleared to the approach. Silence means continue.

**How many pilots on this frequency will do the same?**
All of them. Silence means continue for everyone. If the plan is to stop us landing, somebody has to say so, to each of us, in time to act.

**What would help?**
One instruction, early, with a reason I can tell the cabin. "Divert Boeing Field, hazard on the approach" is enough. I do not need the picture. I need the call.

## Coach observations

- Silence is an instruction. Every aircraft not told otherwise will land into the path. Doing nothing is a decision with a known outcome.
- Pilots will self-divert after a short wait. The window to coordinate diverts is shorter than the clock; it is the pilots' patience.
- "I need the call, not the picture" is the sharpest version of the display rejection in the other interviews.
