# Interview: FAA tower supervisor, Sea-Tac

Label: role-play, not evidence. Pre-seeded by the facilitator. Method 1b.

## Who

Tower supervisor on duty. Last voice to every cockpit before touchdown. Issues landing clearances and runway assignments. Sits above the field, sees the runways and the final approach visually and on the tower display.

## Can see

Runways, aircraft on short final, ground traffic, the tower radar display for the immediate area. Weather at the field.

## Cannot see

Fuel state. Aircraft beyond about 15 km. Vespyra, until it is close enough to be a visual threat. Anything Titan Watch knows.

## Questions and answers

**When a ground stop is issued, what happens in the tower in the first four minutes?**
We stop issuing departure clearances immediately. Arrivals already cleared to land, land. Arrivals on final not yet cleared, we clear them unless told not to. The order comes from the FAA command centre or from approach; it comes over the landline, then we read it on the system a minute later. Four minutes is the time for every cockpit on our frequency to acknowledge. Aircraft on other frequencies, that is approach's problem.

**How many aircraft can you land in 15 minutes?**
Three runways, but 16C and 16R are too close to use for simultaneous arrivals in low visibility. Call it two usable. One a minute per runway on a good day, with ground crews clearing them. Thirty in 15 minutes if nothing goes wrong. Something always goes wrong. Assume and label: twenty.

**What happens to an aircraft that lands and then Vespyra crosses the field?**
I do not know. Nobody has told us whether on the ground is safe. We have been assuming landed means done.

**Who tells you to stop landing aircraft?**
Under normal conditions, nobody. We land what approach gives us. Under a kaiju threat, I would expect the FAA command centre. I have never had an order from Titan Watch and I would not know how to verify one.

**What do you need that you do not have?**
Which aircraft on final has fuel to go around and which does not. Right now, if I need to send one around, I ask the pilot, and the pilot takes ten seconds to answer, and I have four aircraft behind them.

## Coach observations

- "Landed means done" is an assumption (A2) that nobody has checked. If the field is the risk point, the outcome statement is wrong.
- Thirty becomes twenty becomes an unknown number. Q4 stays open with a role-play range.
- The tower would not recognise an order from Titan Watch. Q1 has a verification problem as well as an authority problem.
