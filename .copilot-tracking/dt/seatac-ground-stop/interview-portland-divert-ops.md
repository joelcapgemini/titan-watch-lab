# Interview: Portland airport operations, divert field

Label: role-play, not evidence. Pre-seeded by the facilitator. Method 1b.

## Who

Duty manager at Portland International, the main divert field, 25 minutes' flying south of Sea-Tac.

## Can see

Own runways, gates, ground crews. The national flow picture. Inbound aircraft once they are handed to Portland approach.

## Cannot see

Sea-Tac's picture. Which aircraft are coming until each one is handed over. Vespyra.

## Questions and answers

**How many diverts can you take in the next hour?**
Twelve to fifteen before we run out of gates and people start sitting on taxiways. We can land more than that. We cannot park them. Assume and label: fifteen.

**How much warning do you need?**
Twenty minutes to stage. Less than ten and the first arrivals land into an unprepared airport. Nobody has called us yet.

**Who tells you a divert is coming?**
The airline, usually, once their captain decides. Sometimes approach, once the aircraft is handed over. Never Sea-Tac itself. We find out aircraft by aircraft.

**What happens if six airlines each send you their aircraft without coordinating?**
We take the first fifteen and refuse the rest in the air. The refused ones then have less fuel and fewer options. That is the failure mode.

**What would help?**
One list from Sea-Tac, early: how many, which types, in what order. Even an estimate. We plan against an estimate; we cannot plan against silence.

## Coach observations

- Divert capacity is finite and uncoordinated. Six airlines deciding independently can exceed it, and the overflow is worse off than before.
- Portland hears aircraft by aircraft, never from Sea-Tac. Assumption A3, divert fields open, is true but insufficient: open is not the same as ready.
- "One list, early, even an estimate" matches the fire chief's and the duty manager's asks. Same artefact wanted from three places.
