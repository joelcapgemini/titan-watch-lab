# Interview: FAA command centre duty officer

Label: role-play, not evidence. Pre-seeded by the facilitator. Method 1b.

## Who

National ground stop authority. Sits in a room far from Sea-Tac. Issues and lifts ground stops for weather, volcanic ash, security events. Has never issued one for a kaiju.

## Can see

National traffic picture, flow rates, which airports are stopped. Weather. The Titan Watch public alert, same as everyone.

## Cannot see

Individual aircraft fuel. The local picture at Sea-Tac. Anything Titan Watch has not published.

## Questions and answers

**Who orders a ground stop for a kaiju?**
Us, if someone asks and we agree. The airport can refuse arrivals on its own. Approach can hold aircraft on its own. State Patrol has nothing to do with aircraft. So three people can each stop the flow, and nobody has to. Assume and label: no written procedure.

**How fast?**
Request to order, four to six minutes if the request is clear. Longer if we have to verify who is asking. A call from a Titan Watch liaison we would verify. A call from the tower we would act on.

**What about the aircraft already inside approach airspace?**
A ground stop keeps aircraft on the ground elsewhere. It does nothing for the forty already in the air. Those are approach's problem and the airport's. We can suggest diverts. We cannot sequence.

**Have you drilled this?**
Volcanic ash, twice. Mass diversion worked on paper. Nobody timed it against a clock shorter than an hour.

**What would help?**
One verified voice from Sea-Tac asking for one thing. Today we would get three calls from three agencies saying three things.

## Coach observations

- The ground stop is the wrong tool for the forty aircraft already airborne. The scenario's hard choice is local, not national.
- Verification of who is asking costs minutes. Q1 is an authority problem and an identity problem.
- "Three calls saying three things" is the commander gap, seen from the receiving end.
