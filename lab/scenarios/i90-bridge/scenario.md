# Scenario 2: The bridge is floating

**Slug:** `i90-span-clear` - **Repo:** github.com/joelcapgemini/titan-watch-seatac - **Your role:** incident commander, full authority, next page

## The kaiju, in thirty seconds

- **Terrakon.** Tectonic Behemoth, 120 m, 67,000 t, 22.5 km/h, faster near shore. Walks the lake bed, surfaced. Declared target Mercer Island. The I-90 span is in the way. Does not stop. Cannot be stopped.
- **26 minutes** to the "anchor line". Titan Watch's phrase and figure, straight-line, refreshed every 30 seconds. Usually runs early. Nobody has confirmed what that is.
- **The span is lost twice.** Unsafe for traffic first, then broken. Its anchors feel the footfalls before it arrives.
- **Who sees it.** Satellite sees where it is, every 30 seconds. Observers see what it does, not where. Engineers see strain, not cause. Nobody sees all of it. That gap is the scenario.

4,000 vehicles and maybe two trains are on the span. Clear it and strand Mercer Island, or keep it open and gamble 4,000 vehicles on a cable.

## Outcome, same for every scenario

Save as much human life as possible, then prevent as much public property damage as possible, in that order. A software tool is expected to come out of this.

**You are graded on, in this order:** people on the span when it goes; people stranded on Mercer Island without warning; damage to the bridge and its anchors; and whether every decision you made was a decision, recorded, with a name on it.

The coach interviews you against this, narrows it to this clock and this span, and restates it after synthesis. The original stays in the scope file beside the restatement.
